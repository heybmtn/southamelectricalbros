The short answer

A home battery lets you buy electricity cheap and use it when it's expensive. Overnight EV and smart tariffs charge around 8p per kWh, while standard electricity under the October 2026 price cap is around 26p per kWh. A battery captures that gap every day – and stores your spare solar instead of selling it back for less. With prices forecast to rise again and 0% VAT ending on 31 March 2027, now is the window to act.

5 reasons battery storage matters now

1. Electricity prices are going up again

Ofgem raised the energy price cap by 4% from 1 October 2026, taking a typical dual-fuel bill to £1,723 a year. The House of Commons Library reports a further rise of around 9% is forecast for January–March 2027. A battery shifts more of your usage onto cheap rates, so each rise hurts less.

2. The cheap-rate gap has never been wider

Smart tariffs now offer overnight electricity at around 8p per kWh – roughly a third of the standard capped rate. Every kWh your battery charges overnight and uses at teatime saves about 18p. That adds up fast in a busy household.

3. Selling solar back pays far less than buying it

If you already have solar, flat export rates in 2026 sit around 12–15p per kWh. Buying the same electricity back in the evening costs around 26p. Storing your midday surplus and using it after dark is usually worth roughly double what exporting it earns.

4. 0% VAT ends on 31 March 2027

Batteries are zero-rated for VAT – whether fitted with solar or on their own (standalone batteries have qualified since February 2024). From 1 April 2027 the rate is due to rise to 5%, and the government has not said it will extend the relief.

5. Winter is when a battery works hardest

Solar output drops in winter, but a battery keeps earning by charging overnight on a cheap tariff. That makes it a year-round saving, not just a summer one.

How a home battery saves you money

There are three common setups:

  • Solar + battery – panels charge the battery during the day; your home runs on stored power in the evening instead of buying from the grid.
  • Battery only – no panels needed. The battery charges overnight on a cheap tariff and powers your home through the expensive peak. Ideal if your roof isn't suitable for solar (see our roof suitability guide).
  • Battery + EV – the car charges overnight at the cheap rate, while the battery covers your home's daytime and evening use. See picking the best EV charger.

What size battery do you need?

Bigger isn't always better. The right size is the one you'll actually fill and empty most days.

A useful rule of thumb with solar is roughly 1kWh of storage for every 1kW of panels – but we size from your actual usage, not guesswork.

Costs and savings in 2026

Published UK guides put most installed home batteries between about £3,000 and £10,000, with a typical 10kWh system around £5,000–£8,500. The price depends on the brand, whether you need a new hybrid inverter, backup features and how far the battery sits from your fuse board.

A simple worked example

Charge at 8p, use at 26p = 18p saved per kWh. If your household uses 8kWh from the battery on 300 days a year, that's roughly £430 a year on tariff savings alone – before any solar. Your figure depends on your usage and tariff; we'll work it out properly at survey.

Is a battery right for your home?

A battery usually makes sense if:

  • You already have solar and export a lot in summer
  • You use a lot of electricity in the evening
  • You have (or plan to get) an EV or heat pump
  • You can switch to a time-of-use or EV tariff
  • You want some protection against future price rises

It may be worth waiting if:

  • Your electricity use is very low
  • You're planning to move in the next couple of years
  • Your consumer unit needs replacing anyway – do that first (we can quote both together)

Frequently asked questions

Do I need solar panels to have a battery?

No. A battery can charge overnight on a cheap tariff and power your home at peak times. Standalone batteries also get 0% VAT.

Will it keep my lights on in a power cut?

Only if it's set up for backup. Some systems power essential circuits, some the whole home, and some switch off for safety. If backup matters, tell us and we'll specify it.

Can I add a battery to my existing solar?

Yes. An AC-coupled battery works with most existing systems. If your inverter is ageing, a hybrid inverter can be the better option – we'll advise.

How much does a home battery cost?

Most are around £3,000–£10,000 installed in 2026, with a 10kWh system typically £5,000–£8,500. VAT is currently 0%.

How long do batteries last?

Most modern home batteries use LFP cells and carry 10–15 year warranties.
