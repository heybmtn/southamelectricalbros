The short answer

For most Oxfordshire homes, the best choice is a 7kW smart charger that works with a cheap overnight EV tariff. Choose tethered (cable attached) for convenience or untethered for a tidier look and future flexibility. If you have solar, pick one that can charge from your spare solar power. The real deciding factors are your electrical supply, parking position and cable run – which is why a proper survey matters.

The 6 decisions that matter

1. Charging speed: 7kW is right for almost everyone

Nearly all homes have a single-phase supply, which caps home charging at around 7kW – roughly 25–30 miles of range per hour. Plug in at 11pm and most cars are full by morning. 22kW chargers need a three-phase supply (rare in homes) and a car that can accept 22kW, so they're usually only worth it for businesses.

2. Tethered or untethered?

Tethered chargers have the cable permanently attached – grab and go. Untethered chargers have a socket, so you use your own cable. Untethered looks neater on period frontages in Burford or Woodstock and suits households that might change car; tethered is easier day to day.

3. Tariff compatibility

All new home chargers must be "smart" by law, but not every smart charger works with every tariff. The cheapest EV tariffs control your charging directly and only support certain chargers or cars. Check which tariff you want first, then choose a charger on its approved list.

4. Solar matching

If you have (or plan to get) solar panels, a solar-matching charger can top up your car with surplus power that would otherwise be exported for much less. It pairs well with a home battery – see why solar battery storage matters now.

5. Your electrical supply and fuse board

A 7kW charger draws around 32 amps – a big load for an older house. We check your main fuse size, consumer unit and earthing. Where the supply is tight, we fit load management so the charger automatically slows down when the oven, shower and heating are on. We also handle the notification to your network operator.

6. Position and looks

Put the charger where the cable reaches your car's charging port without stretching across paths. Wall-mount is standard; a post-mounted unit works for detached garages or long rural driveways. In conservation areas, a discreet colour and position helps.

Your property: driveway, garage or no driveway

Driveways on newer estates

Most homes on the newer estates around Carterton, Witney and Eynsham are straightforward: a short cable run from the consumer unit through an external wall to a charger by the drive. These installs are often done in half a day.

Detached garages and long rural driveways

Around Burford, Charlbury and Chipping Norton we often run armoured cable underground to a garage or post-mounted charger. Longer runs need correctly sized cable and careful earthing – get this right once and it'll last decades.

No driveway? Oxfordshire's cable channel scheme

Oxfordshire County Council runs an EV cable channel scheme: a channel set into the pavement that lets your cable run safely from a home charger to your car at the kerb. The council's rules include:

  • You must apply to the council – you can't install a channel yourself
  • The home charger must be 7.5kW or lower, with a cable no more than 16mm thick
  • You'll need planning permission from your district council for the charger itself
  • Not every street qualifies – parking restrictions and verges can rule it out
  • Never trail a cable across the pavement, even under a mat

This is especially relevant in Oxford and Abingdon terraces. Get council approval first, then we'll fit a compliant charger to your front wall.

EV charger grants in 2026

Renting or living in a flat? You could get up to £500 off

The OZEV EV chargepoint grant for renters and flat owners rose to up to £500 per socket for applications from 1 April 2026. That includes many rented homes around Carterton and RAF Brize Norton (you'll need your landlord's permission). Homeowners of houses aren't currently eligible.

Key rules to follow so you don't lose the grant:

  • Get a dated quote from an OZEV-authorised installer first
  • Get any landlord or freeholder permission before applying
  • Apply through the government's Find a Grant service
  • Don't install until OZEV confirms you're eligible

Frequently asked questions

Is there a grant for a home EV charger in 2026?

Yes, if you rent or own a flat – up to £500 per socket from 1 April 2026. Homeowners of houses aren't currently eligible. Don't install until OZEV confirms your eligibility.

Should I get a 7kW or 22kW charger?

7kW for almost every home. 22kW needs a three-phase supply and a compatible car.

Can I charge at home without a driveway?

Possibly, through Oxfordshire County Council's cable channel scheme. You apply to the council, use a charger of 7.5kW or less, and get planning permission for the charger.

How much does it cost to charge at home?

Around £3.40 to add 42kWh on an 8p overnight tariff, versus about £11 at the standard capped rate.

Will my fuse board cope?

Often yes, but we always check. Where the supply is tight we fit load management so nothing overloads.
