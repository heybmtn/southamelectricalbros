The short answer

Most homes in Carterton, Witney and the surrounding villages can take solar. The best roofs face south, east or west, have roughly 15–20m² of unshaded space, are in good condition with at least 10 years' life left, and aren't on a listed building. North-facing or heavily shaded roofs are the main reasons we'd advise against it – and even then there are often alternatives.

The 7-point roof check

You can do most of this from your garden or on Google Maps satellite view. Tick off as many as you can, then send us what you find.

1. Which way does your roof face?

South-facing roofs produce the most electricity over a year. East- and west-facing roofs produce a bit less in total but spread generation into mornings and evenings – often a better match for families and people working from home. Many of the newer estates around Carterton and Witney have east/west roofs, and split systems across both slopes work well. North-facing roofs are rarely worth it on their own.

2. What's the pitch?

Around 30–40° is ideal, which covers most pitched roofs in the area. Shallower roofs and flat roofs (garages, extensions, dormers) still work using angled mounting frames.

3. Is there enough space?

A typical 4kW home system is around 10 modern panels, needing roughly 20m² of clear roof. Smaller arrays are fine – they just generate less. Velux windows, vents and chimneys eat into usable space, so measure the uninterrupted areas.

4. Does anything shade it?

Trees, chimneys, dormers and neighbouring buildings all matter – especially between 10am and 4pm. Partial shade isn't a deal-breaker: panel optimisers or microinverters stop one shaded panel dragging down the rest. In leafy villages like Charlbury and Woodstock, we check shading in winter too, when the sun is lower.

5. What condition is the roof in?

Panels last 25+ years. If your roof needs recovering in the next few years, do that first – removing and refitting panels later costs money. Slipped tiles, sagging ridges or failing felt should be fixed before install.

6. What's it made of?

This is where Oxfordshire gets interesting – see roof types below.

7. Are your electrics ready?

This is the step most solar sellers skip – and it's the one we care about most as electricians. We check your consumer unit (fuse board), earthing and space for an isolator and generation meter. Older boards may need upgrading. We also handle the notification to your local network operator so your system is connected properly.

Planning rules in West Oxfordshire & the Cotswolds

For most houses, roof-mounted solar is permitted development – no planning application needed – as long as panels don't sit above the highest part of the roof and project no more than 200mm from the slope.

Things get more careful in parts of our patch:

  • Listed buildings always need listed building consent – common in Burford, Woodstock and Chipping Norton.
  • Conservation areas (including the historic centres of Witney, Burford, Woodstock, Chipping Norton and Charlbury) have extra conditions, such as no panels on walls facing a road.
  • Article 4 directions can remove permitted development rights on specific streets.
  • The Cotswolds National Landscape covers much of the area north and west of Carterton, where visual impact gets more attention for anything prominent or ground-mounted.
The rules changed in August 2026

The national permitted development rules for solar on houses were rewritten on 27 August 2026, with a 12-month transition period running to 27 August 2027. We check your address against the current rules with the right council – West Oxfordshire District Council, Vale of White Horse, or Oxford City Council – before we quote.

What if your roof isn't suitable?

A "no" on the roof isn't always a "no" on solar:

  • Garage, outbuilding or extension roofs – often south-facing and easier to work on.
  • Ground-mounted panels – great for larger gardens and rural properties around Burford and Charlbury.
  • Plug-in (balcony) solar – legal in Great Britain since 27 August 2026. Kits are limited to 800W output and one per household. They're modest, and not every home's wiring suits them – for example, some older RCDs are unsuitable. If you're considering one, we can check your electrics first.
  • A battery on its own – no panels needed. Charge on a cheap overnight tariff and use it at peak times. See our guide: why solar battery storage matters now.

Why now: VAT and energy prices

Two things make autumn and winter 2026 a good time to get your roof assessed:

  • 0% VAT ends on 31 March 2027. Home solar and batteries are currently zero-rated. From 1 April 2027 the rate is due to rise to 5%, and the government hasn't said it will extend the relief.
  • Electricity prices are rising again. Ofgem's price cap went up 4% from 1 October 2026, and further rises are forecast for early 2027.

Surveys, design and any planning checks take time, so booking a roof check now gives you room to install well before the VAT deadline – and get panels up ready for spring, when generation picks up.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need planning permission for solar panels in West Oxfordshire?

Most homes don't. Roof-mounted solar on a house is usually permitted development. Listed buildings always need consent, and some conservation areas have Article 4 directions. Because the rules changed on 27 August 2026, we check every address with the council before quoting.

Can you put solar panels on a Cotswold stone roof?

Sometimes. Stone slates are heavy and crack easily, so standard fixings aren't always right. We'll often look at a rear slope, an outbuilding or a ground-mount instead – and check planning first, as many stone-roofed homes are listed.

Are east- or west-facing roofs worth it?

Usually, yes. You'll generate a little less over the year than a south-facing roof, but more of it arrives in the morning and evening when most homes use power.

Is there VAT on solar panels in 2026?

No – home solar and batteries are zero-rated until 31 March 2027. From 1 April 2027 the rate is due to go back to 5%.

How long does installation take?

Most homes take one to two days once scaffolding is up.
